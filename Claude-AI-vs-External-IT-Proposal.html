1Executive summary

Canadian Royalties spends on the order of $3.76M per year across external IT consultants, contractors and suppliers. Most of that is not replaceable by AI — it is physical hardware, field installation, satellite/radio infrastructure, and licensed platforms that must continue. But a meaningful slice — roughly $1.1M of knowledge work (business analysis, custom development, data integration, BI dashboards, and document/invoice automation) — is exactly the kind of work Claude AI does well.

Our recommendation is deliberately "replace where it makes sense", not wholesale replacement. Claude should absorb the routine, repeatable knowledge work — freeing the company from paying consultant day-rates for dashboard builds, data plumbing, internal app tweaks and invoice processing — while specialist vendors are retained for OT/SCADA engineering, HRIS/payroll platforms, computer vision, and all field and hardware work.

Bottom line. A Claude Team/Enterprise deployment costing $20–125K/year can credibly displace $170–430K/year of consultant knowledge-work, for a net saving of roughly $150–305K in year one and more thereafter — while keeping the intellectual property and institutional knowledge in-house rather than walking out the door with a departing consultant.

3How we judged "replaceable"

Each vendor was scored against a simple test: is the work knowledge work that an AI assistant plus a small internal team can do, or does it require something Claude fundamentally cannot provide? Three verdicts result:

REPLACE

Routine, repeatable knowledge work — dashboards, reports, data integration scripts, internal forms/apps, document and invoice processing. Claude can do the bulk of this directly; consultant day-rates fall away.

AUGMENT

Work tied to a specialist platform or domain (HRIS, CMMS, SCADA) where the licence and core expertise stay, but Claude cuts the hours spent on configuration, migration, testing, documentation and integration glue.

KEEP

Physical hardware, field installation and maintenance, satellite/radio infrastructure, real-time control systems, computer vision, and vendor licences. Claude adds little or nothing to the core deliverable; these vendors are retained in full.

A note on honesty. Capture percentages below are planning estimates, not guarantees. They assume the company stands up a small internal "AI-enabled IT" capability to operate Claude — without that, savings will not materialise. They should be validated against one or two real pilots before any contract is reduced.

5What Claude replaces — and what it doesn't

Claude is a strong fit for

  • Power BI & reporting dashboard development
  • Data integration / ETL scripts across applications
  • Business analysis & requirements documentation
  • Internal app, form and workflow building (HR, H&S, environment)
  • Invoice / document processing & 3-way-match automation
  • Data clean-up (parts catalogues, price lists, migrations)
  • Test-case generation & user documentation for platform rollouts
  • Rapid, low-cost proofs-of-concept before big commitments

Claude does not replace

  • Field installation & maintenance (LTE, towers, fuel, UPS)
  • Hardware supply (Dell, radios, satellite terminals)
  • Licensed platforms (AVEVA, DLGL VIP, Guide TI, M365, Veeam, Cisco)
  • OT / SCADA real-time control & industrial engineering
  • Real-time computer vision & edge AI (Always AI)
  • Satellite / radio / microwave network infrastructure
  • Regulatory-grade payroll processing (system of record)
The honest framing: Claude doesn't shrink the IT budget by half. It removes a specific, recurring cost — paying outside firms premium day-rates to build and maintain software, dashboards and data flows that a small, Claude-equipped internal team can increasingly own.

Enablement is a year-1 cost (building agents, skills, integrations and training). From year 2 onward it largely falls away, so steady-state net savings are higher — roughly $165K / $270K / $385K for the three scenarios. Base-case year-1 return on Claude spend is about 3.8×.

9Recommendation & phased rollout

Adopt Claude to insource the knowledge-work layer of IT — start small, prove it on two pilots, then reduce consultant scope on evidence. Do not touch field contractors, hardware suppliers, licensed platforms or OT specialists.
